*Extent · 295 từ · điểm AI: 7.5*

**Cyberbullying, online scams, false news and loneliness** are regularly blamed on the internet, and **some argue that** it is **the main reason for social problems**. I disagree **to a large extent**, since the internet **magnifies social problems but rarely creates them**, and its benefits are also **considerable**.

**To begin with**, the internet **magnifies social problems but rarely creates them**. Bullying, misinformation and isolation existed before, and the internet makes them **faster and more visible**, while the underlying causes are **poverty, family breakdown and weak education**. A false rumour about a neighbour, **for example**, once **spread slowly by gossip** but can now **reach thousands in minutes**. This amplification is serious, since **victims may face abuse at any hour**, but the roots of the behaviour lie in **human attitudes and social conditions**, not in cables and servers. **Crime, inequality, addiction and discrimination** all existed long before the internet and still exist in places where few people are online.

**Furthermore**, the internet also helps to solve social problems. It allows **isolated people** to find communities, helps victims to **report abuse**, gives **access to education and health information**, and enables campaigns for change. During crises, such as pandemics, online tools **kept schools, businesses and families connected**. However, it would be wrong to ignore real harm. **Algorithms that reward outrage** can **increase division**, and heavy use may affect the wellbeing of some **young people**. Governments and platforms should therefore regulate **harmful** content, **protect children and teach digital skills**, while also tackling the **social conditions** that make people **vulnerable**. Without addressing these deeper causes, restricting the internet alone would solve little.

**In conclusion**, the internet is not **the main reason for social problems**, because it mostly amplifies difficulties that have other causes, although it can **aggravate them** and needs sensible regulation.

### #173 — How far do you think wearable technology will change personal health monitoring in the next 20 years? Support your answer with reasons.
