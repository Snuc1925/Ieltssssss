With only a laptop and an internet connection, students in remote villages can attend live lessons, **revisit recorded explanations** and study at times that **suit their circumstances**. The **issue** of **low participation** in **adult education** is **a matter of growing concern**. This essay will **analyse the principal causes** and propose realistic solutions.

**Financial barriers** **play a substantial part in** **low participation** in **adult education**. Fees, living costs and **the loss of income while studying** discourage many people from **returning to education**, particularly those **from modest backgrounds**. **In addition**, **low awareness and motivation** **contribute to** this problem. Where people see little connection between study and **tangible rewards**, or are **held back by stereotypes and busy schedules**, they simply **do not engage**. **Working adults** with **family commitments** are typical examples, as they must **balance jobs, childcare and household tasks**. A single parent who works two jobs, **for example**, has **neither the money nor the energy** for **evening classes**.

To address this **issue**, provision should be made more flexible and **affordable**. **Subsidised fees**, evening and online courses, and grants towards living costs allow people with **limited money or time** to participate **without sacrificing their income**. Barriers fall **as a result**, and **participation rises** among groups that were **previously excluded**. **In addition**, **awareness campaigns** and **visible role models** would **change attitudes towards learning**. Media campaigns, mentoring by successful adult **learners** and support from employers, such as **paid study leave**, show people that study leads to **rewarding careers**. Once these links become clear, **motivation tends to follow**, and workplaces can **become partners in training**.

**In conclusion**, **low participation** in **adult education** **stems mainly from** **financial barriers** and low awareness, but flexible and **affordable** provision and **awareness campaigns** **offer a realistic way forward**.
