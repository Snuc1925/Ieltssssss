### #49 — What are the causes of crime in large cities? What solutions can be proposed?

*Causes / Solutions · 282 từ · điểm AI: 7.0*

Large cities offer opportunities and excitement, but many of them also suffer from high levels of **theft, violence and drug-related crime**. This essay will **analyse the principal causes** and propose realistic solutions.

Crime in large cities is **driven by inequality, unemployment and weak social ties**. Where some people see wealth around them but have **no job or prospects**, and where **neighbours do not know one another**, **gangs, drugs and theft become more attractive** and **harder to detect**. A teenager who **leaves school with no qualifications** and no job, **for example**, may be drawn by **a gang that offers money and belonging**. **In addition**, **crowded housing**, poor schools and the **easy availability of drugs and alcohol** increase the risk, and **the anonymity of city life** makes offenders **feel safe from being recognised**. Families may also be **under strain**, leaving **young people** with little guidance. **As a result**, crime tends to **concentrate in a few deprived districts**.

To address this **issue**, governments should tackle the causes of crime as well as punish offenders. Jobs, education and youth programmes give people **a stake in society**, **better lighting and street design** make public places safer, and **treatment for addiction** reduces the crimes that feed it. **In addition**, **community policing**, in which officers get to know the residents they serve, helps to build trust and gives early warning of problems. Faster and fairer courts, together with support for former prisoners to find work, **reduce reoffending**. These measures cost less than building more prisons, and the improvement lasts longer.

**In conclusion**, crime in large cities **stems mainly from** inequality, unemployment and weak community ties, but investment in opportunity, safer design and **community policing** offers a realistic way forward.
