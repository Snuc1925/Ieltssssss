**First and foremost**, media coverage **decides which issues voters think about** and how they see candidates. By choosing **what to report, how to frame it and whom to interview**, newspapers and broadcasters **shape priorities**. If the media spend weeks on immigration and little on health care, voters may come to feel that immigration is **the main issue**, whatever their own concerns. **To sum up**, media coverage strongly affects public opinion during elections by shaping priorities and images, so diversity and honesty in the media are **essential** to democracy.

### #378 — Discuss how language policy can affect national unity.

**To begin with**, compelling people to vote **treats a right as a duty** and may **produce uninformed choices**. **Citizens** who are **forced to attend a polling station** may vote randomly or **leave the paper blank**, **resent the state** and lose the sense that voting is **a free expression of opinion**. Supporters point out that compulsory voting **increases turnout** and makes governments **more representative**, as in Australia, where **participation is very high**. That is a real advantage. However, the same result can be achieved by making voting easier and more meaningful through **weekend elections, early and postal voting**, better civic education and **more responsive politics**. Fines also **fall hardest on those with the least money**.

**In conclusion**, I disagree that **voting should be compulsory**, because it limits freedom and produces uninformed votes, and the focus should be on making **participation** easier and more attractive.
